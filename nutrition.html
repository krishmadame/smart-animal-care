Animal Nutrition Guide

Scientifically balanced feeding guidelines, green and dry fodder ratios, mineral supplementation, and clean water management for optimal livestock health.

🌾

Important Nutritional Notice

Feeding requirements can vary depending on factors such as age, body weight, health status, production stage (lactating, pregnant, dry), breed, and local environmental conditions. Consult a qualified veterinarian or animal nutrition professional for farm-specific ration formulation.

The 5 Pillars of Animal Nutrition

A successful livestock diet combines energy, protein, digestible fiber, minerals, and ad-libitum clean water.

🌱

1. Green Fodder (हरा चारा)

Rich in natural carotene (Vitamin A precursor), succulence, and minerals.

  • Legumes (High Protein): Berseem, Lucerne, Cowpea, Stylo.
  • Cereals / Grasses (High Energy): Maize, Sorghum (Jowar), Bajra, Hybrid Napier (CO-4/CO-5).
  • Silage: Fermented corn stalks preserve nutrients for dry seasons.
🌾

2. Dry Fodder & Straw (सूखा चारा)

Provides essential structural crude fiber required for healthy rumination, saliva buffering, and butterfat synthesis.

  • Wheat Straw (भूसा): Excellent dry roughage base.
  • Paddy Straw: Soak or treat with urea-molasses to boost protein availability.
  • Chaffing: Always chaff straw into 1-2 inch lengths to prevent waste and save animal chewing energy.
🥣

3. Balanced Concentrates (पशु आहार)

Dense energy and protein blend required to meet the high metabolic demands of milk synthesis and growth.

  • Energy Grains (35-40%): Maize, barley, broken wheat.
  • Protein Oil Cakes (30-35%): Mustard, cottonseed, groundnut, or soybean meal.
  • Brans & Fiber (25-30%): Wheat bran, rice polish, or de-oiled cakes.
🧂

4. Minerals & Common Salt

Essential for fertility, estrus expression, immune defense, and milk let-down.

  • Daily mineral mixture: 50-60g for cattle/buffalo; 10-15g for goat/sheep.
  • Hang mineralized salt licks in sheds for free-choice trace mineral intake.
  • Proper 2:1 Calcium to Phosphorus ratio prevents Milk Fever post-calving.
💧

5. Clean Drinking Water (स्वच्छ पानी)

Milk is 87% water! Water is the cheapest yet most frequently neglected nutrient on the farm.

  • Dairy cows need 3-4 liters of water for every liter of milk produced.
  • Water must be free from coliforms, heavy nitrates, and algal toxins.
  • Scrub drinking troughs weekly and provide cool shade over water tanks.
⏱️

Golden Feeding Practices

Consistent feeding routines maximize ruminal fermentation efficiency.

  • Never change diets abruptly; transition gradually over 7 to 10 days.
  • Feed concentrates in two equal halves (morning and evening milking).
  • Clean the feeding manger daily; remove stale, saliva-soaked, or moldy feed.

Animal-Specific Feeding Guidelines

Explore specialized nutritional strategies designed for cows, buffalos, goats, and sheep.

🐄 Dairy Cow Feeding Management

High Milk Yield Focus

A high-yielding cow requires steady energy to avoid negative energy balance in early lactation (which triggers ketosis). Feed 1 kg concentrate for every 2.5-3.0 liters of milk, plus a 1.0-1.5 kg maintenance allowance. Mix leguminous and non-leguminous green fodder in a 1:3 ratio for balanced rumen crude protein and energy.

Production Level Green Fodder (kg) Dry Straw (kg) Concentrate (kg) Mineral Mix
Dry / Non-Milking (400 kg) 15 - 20 kg 5 - 6 kg 1.0 - 1.5 kg 30 - 40 grams
Medium Milking (8 - 12 L/day) 25 - 30 kg 4 - 5 kg 3.5 - 4.5 kg 50 grams
High Milking (15+ L/day) 30 - 35 kg 4 kg 5.5 - 7.0 kg 60 - 80 grams

🐃 Water Buffalo Feeding Management

High Fat Secretion Focus

Buffaloes excel at digesting lignified, fibrous crop residues. However, because buffalo milk contains 7-8% butterfat, energy requirements per liter of milk are substantially higher than cattle. Feed 1 kg concentrate for every 2.0 liters of buffalo milk. During summer, provide cool drinking water and night-time stall feeding when ambient temperatures drop.

Production Level Green Fodder (kg) Dry Straw (kg) Concentrate (kg) Mineral Mix
Dry / Maintenance (500 kg) 20 kg 6 - 7 kg 1.5 kg 40 grams
Lactating (8 - 12 L/day) 30 - 35 kg 5 - 6 kg 4.5 - 6.0 kg 60 grams
Advanced Gestation (7+ months) 25 kg 5 kg 2.5 - 3.0 kg 60 grams

🐐 Goat Feeding & Browsing Management

Selective Browser

Goats possess a mobile split upper lip and prefer browsing tree foliage (Subabul, Neem, Ber, Gliricidia, Sesbania) over pasture grasses. They have a higher dry matter requirement (3.5% to 4.0% of body weight) due to rapid digestive transit. Provide clean water in buckets elevated off the ground to prevent defecation inside water.

Adult Doe (Maintenance, 35 kg) 2.5 - 3.5 kg 0.5 - 1.0 kg 150 - 200 grams 3 - 5 Liters
Milking Doe (2 L/day) 4.0 - 5.0 kg 0.8 kg 400 - 500 grams 6 - 8 Liters
Growing Kid (3 - 6 months) 1.5 kg tender greens Ad-lib hay 150 - 250 grams 1 - 2 Liters

🐑 Sheep Feeding & Grazing Management

Grazer & Flock Management

Sheep are close-grazers that thrive on natural grasslands and stubble. Flushing (offering 200g grain supplement for 2 weeks prior to breeding) increases ovulation rates and twinning percentage. Caution: Ensure mineral supplements are formulated specifically for sheep with low copper levels to prevent lethal chronic copper poisoning.

Dry Ewe (30 kg) 6 - 8 hrs grazing 0.5 kg hay Optional 100g Maintenance pasture
Flushing / Late Gestation 6 - 8 hrs grazing 0.8 kg 250 - 300 grams High energy grain flush
Lactating Ewe (Nursing Lamb) 3 - 4 kg green fodder 0.8 kg 350 - 400 grams Ensures abundant milk
