Medical & Diagnostic Disclaimer

This information is for educational purposes only and should not be used as a substitute for professional veterinary diagnosis or treatment. Never administer prescription antibiotics or surgical procedures without veterinary supervision. If your animal is in acute pain, stops eating, or shows high fever, immediately contact a registered veterinarian.

Viral Outbreak

Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD)

Vernacular: खुरपका-मुंहपका रोग
High Contagion
Affects: Cow Buffalo Goat Sheep
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
High Fever (104°-106°F) Excessive Ropy Salivation Blisters on Tongue & Gums Interdigital Hoof Lesions Severe Lameness Dramatic Milk Drop
🛡️ Prevention:

Bi-annual vaccination (pre-monsoon and post-monsoon) under the National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP). Quarantine new herd entries for 21 days. Disinfect shed gates with 4% sodium carbonate.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Wash oral lesions with 1% potassium permanganate or alum solution. Apply boro-glycerine to mouth ulcers and apply neem/antiseptic ointment to hoof wounds to repel maggots. Provide soft gruel feed.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Report outbreak immediately to the local government dispensary. Calves under 6 months can die suddenly from myocardial failure (tiger heart) without showing blisters.

Acute Bacterial

Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS)

Vernacular: गलघोंटू रोग (Pasteurellosis)
High Fatality
Affects: Buffalo (Highly Sensitive) Affects: Cow
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Very High Fever (106°-107°F) Swollen Hot Throat & Brisket Severe Labored Grunting Breath Swollen Tongue Protruding Profuse Salivation Collapse within 24 Hours
🛡️ Prevention:

Annual vaccination with HS alum-precipitated vaccine strictly prior to monsoon onset (May-June). Avoid working draught animals in heavy rain or swampy pastures during outbreaks.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Isolate the sick animal in a cool, dry area. Keep the head elevated to ease airway obstruction. Do not force-drench liquids as fluid can enter the lungs due to pharyngeal swelling.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

EMERGENCY! Death can occur within 12 to 36 hours. A veterinarian must immediately administer IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (sulfa drugs / oxytetracycline) in the earliest febrile stage.

Lactation Infection

Bovine Mastitis

Vernacular: थनैला रोग (Udder Inflammation)
Economic Loss
Affects: High-Yielding Cow Buffalo
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Hot, Hard, Swollen Quarter Curd-like Flakes or Clots in Milk Watery or Blood-Stained Milk Cow Kicking During Milking Fever & Loss of Appetite
🛡️ Prevention:

Dry cow therapy. Practice teat dipping with iodine post-milking. Keep milking platform spotless. Ensure cows stand for at least 30-45 minutes after milking by offering fresh feed while teat canals seal.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Perform frequent strip milking of the infected quarter (every 2 hours) and discard infected milk safely. Apply cold compresses or ice packs to reduce acute swelling.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Immediately at the first sign of clots or udder hardness. Delay causes permanent quarter fibrosis or gangrenous mastitis. Antibiotic susceptibility testing is recommended before intramammary infusions.

Vector-Borne Viral

Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD)

Vernacular: लंपी त्वचा रोग
High Morbidity
Affects: Cow (Severe in Indigenous & Crossbreds) Buffalo
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Firm Round Nodules (2-5cm) on Skin High Fever (105°F) Watery Eyes & Nasal Discharge Swollen Legs & Dewlap Edema Deep Ulcers (Sit-fasts)
🛡️ Prevention:

Heterologous vaccination using Goat Pox vaccine (or Lumpi-ProVacInd). Stringent fly, mosquito, and tick control using neem oil sprays, fly traps, and biosecurity isolation.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Clean burst nodules with betadine solution and apply herbal fly-repellent ointments. Provide tender green grass, electrolyte water, and immunity boosters (turmeric, amla, vitamin E & selenium).

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Seek veterinary care for anti-inflammatory injections to control pain and secondary bacterial pneumonia. Respiratory distress and throat nodules require emergency care.

Spore Bacterial

Black Quarter (BQ)

Vernacular: लंगड़ा बुखार / जहरबाद
Rapid Onset
Affects: Cattle (6 months - 2 years young stock) Buffalo
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Sudden Severe Lameness Hot Swelling on Thigh or Shoulder Crepitus (Cracking Sound on Pressing) High Initial Fever (105°F) Dry, Cold Skin over Swelling
🛡️ Prevention:

Annual vaccination of young calves (above 6 months) in May before monsoon rains. Avoid grazing over excavated pasture soils where Clostridium chauvoei spores persist for decades.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Strictly isolate the animal. Keep bedding soft. Never incise the swelling yourself as escaping spores can contaminate the soil permanently for other livestock.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Emergency intervention is needed within hours. Penicillin group antibiotics must be injected parenterally and infiltrated around the muscle lesion by a vet before toxemia sets in.

Contagious Viral

Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR)

Vernacular: बकरी प्लेग (Goat Plague)
Deadly in Goats
Affects: Goat (Extremely Sensitive) Sheep
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Sudden High Fever (104°-106°F) Erosive Ulcers on Gums & Lips Foul-Smelling Watery Diarrhea Crusted Matted Eyes & Nose Pneumonia & Rapid Breathing
🛡️ Prevention:

PPR live-attenuated vaccine administered at 3-4 months of age provides lasting immunity for 3 years. Quarantine newly bought goats from livestock markets for 15 days before introducing to flock.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Clean crusty eyes and nostrils with warm saline. Clean oral erosions with 0.1% potassium permanganate solution. Provide warm oral rehydration solution (ORS) with electrolytes to combat dehydration.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Mortality can reach 80% in unprotected goat flocks. Immediate veterinary administration of fluids, broad-spectrum antibiotics for secondary pneumonia, and anti-inflammatories is crucial.

Digestive Emergency

Acute Bloat / Ruminal Tympany

Vernacular: अफारा (Rumen Gas Distension)
Immediate Risk
Affects: Cow Buffalo Goat Sheep
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Distended Left Flank (Drum-like) Restlessness & Kicking at Belly Mouth Breathing & Protruding Tongue Panting with Frequent Urination Grunting on Exhalation
🛡️ Prevention:

Never turn hungry animals out onto lush dewy legume pasture (alfalfa/berseem). Feed dry straw prior to green forage. Avoid sudden changes in grain rations or access to moldy carbohydrate garbage.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Tie a wooden bit in the mouth to encourage chewing and salivation. Walk the animal slowly uphill. Administer 200-300 ml of vegetable cooking oil (mustard or groundnut oil) or antifoaming bloat drench.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

CRITICAL EMERGENCY: Severe bloat compresses the lungs and diaphragm, causing asphyxiation within 1-2 hours. A vet must relieve pressure via stomach tube or trocar-cannula in the left paralumbar fossa.

Peracute Zoonosis

Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis)

Vernacular: चिरचिटा / गिल्टी रोग
Notifiable Disease
Affects: Cow Buffalo Goat Sheep
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Sudden Death with No Prior Signs Dark Tar-like Non-clotting Blood from Orifices Absence of Rigor Mortis (Stiffness) Severe Bloating Post-Mortem
🛡️ Prevention:

Annual spore vaccination in known endemic zones. Strictly NEVER open or skin a carcass suspected of Anthrax; exposure to air triggers spore formation which contaminates land for 50+ years.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Do not touch body fluids! Deeply bury dead animals (at least 6-8 feet deep) sealed with quicklime, or arrange sanitary carcass incineration.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Notify government veterinary authorities immediately. It is dangerous to human handlers (zoonotic). Surviving herd contacts require urgent post-exposure prophylaxis.

Protozoan / Tick

Babesiosis (Redwater Fever)

Vernacular: चिचड़ी बुखार / लाल पेशाब
High Fever
Affects: Cow (Crossbreds Highly Susceptible) Buffalo
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
High Fever (105°-107°F) Coffee / Reddish-Brown Urine Pale or Yellowish Eye Mucosa (Jaundice) Extreme Weakness & Tremors Heavy Tick Infestation
🛡️ Prevention:

Regular acaricide spraying (Deltamethrin, Amitraz) on animals and shed walls/cracks. Eliminate tick vectors, shear overgrown hair, and avoid introducing tick-laden cattle into clean herds.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Keep the sick animal in shade. Offer fresh clean water with molasses/jaggery for glucose support and fresh soft green grass. Remove ticks manually using gloves and tweezers.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Rapid red blood cell destruction causes fatal anemia. A veterinarian must immediately administer specific antiprotozoal drugs (Diminazene aceturate or Imidocarb dipropionate) along with hematinics.

Clostridial Toxin

Enterotoxemia (Pulpy Kidney)

Vernacular: फिड़किया रोग (Overeating Disease)
Peracute Course
Affects: Sheep Goat
🔍 Common Signs & Symptoms:
Sudden Death of Best Thriving Lambs Convulsions & Head Retraction (Opisthotonos) Frothing at Mouth Teeth Grinding & Incoordination
🛡️ Prevention:

Vaccinate pregnant ewes/does 2-4 weeks before lambing and kids/lambs at 2 months of age. Avoid sudden changes to heavy cereal grains or rich pasture flush without adaptation.

💊 Supportive First-Aid:

Immediately withhold rich concentrates and lush forage. Provide high-fiber coarse hay. Feed oral antacids or baking soda solution to reduce ruminal acidity.

🚨 When to Contact a Veterinarian:

Toxins produced by Clostridium perfringens act rapidly. In early stages, a veterinarian can inject hyperimmune antiserum, pain relievers, and protective antitoxins.
